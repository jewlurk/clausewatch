How to use Clausewatch

Five minutes to set up. After that it runs without you until something changes.

What problem this solves

When MAS amends a notice it republishes the whole PDF with a new date. It does not publish a redline. So finding out what actually changed means putting two long PDFs side by side, and working out which of your internal policies are now out of date means going through your control register by hand.

Clausewatch does the first part automatically and keeps a record of the second, so a change to a clause you depend on reaches you instead of waiting to be found.

Setting up

1. Sign in

Enter your work email on the console. You get a link that signs you straight in — there is no password to choose, forget, or rotate. An organisation is created for you on first sign-in.

2. Follow the instruments that apply to you

On the Watchlist tab, click the instruments your licence brings you under. Most firms need one or two:

Consultancies acting for several clients can follow all of them.

Clausewatch covers every active MAS AML/CFT notice. Notice 3001 (money-changers and remittance) and PSOA-N02 (stored value facilities) are not listed because MAS has cancelled both — the Payment Services Act regime replaced them, and PSN01 and PSN02 cover it.

3. Map your controls — this is the part that pays off

On the Control mappings tab, paste your control register. One per line, three fields:

INTERNAL REF, INSTRUMENT, CLAUSE

For example:

AML-POL-4.2, Notice 626, 6.14
SOP-Onboarding-11, PSN01, 6.7
AML-POL-9, SFA04-N02, 8.1

Paste the whole register at once — it is built for a few hundred lines, not one at a time. Anything Clausewatch cannot match is reported back rather than silently dropped.

Why bother. A watchlist tells you an instrument changed. A mapping tells you which of your documents is now wrong — that clause 6.14 changed and your AML-POL-4.2 is the policy that cites it. That is the difference between a news alert and a task.

Reading a change

Every change shows the same four things:

What you seeWhat it means
AddedA clause that did not exist before
ModifiedWording changed within an existing clause
RemovedThe clause is gone
Renumbered Only the number moved; wording is identical. Usually safe to ignore, but worth checking if your policy cites the number
Obligation The change alters what someone is required to do — these are the ones to read first

Each change leads with a plain-English sentence describing what changed. Under it, Exact wording opens the word-level comparison: added text is highlighted, removed text is struck through, and long unchanged stretches are collapsed so you see the change rather than the whole clause.

Every change links to the official MAS page. Check anything material there before you act on it.

What to do when something changes

  1. Read the summary. If it is not marked Obligation, it is usually a definition, a cross-reference, or a clarification.
  2. Open Exact wording to see precisely what moved.
  3. Check the effective date — MAS often gives months of notice, and sometimes staggers commencement across clauses.
  4. Open the linked MAS page to confirm against the official text.
  5. Pull up the internal document named in the mapping and decide whether it needs updating.

What it does not do

Being straight about the limits, because the gaps matter more than the features:

  • It is not legal advice. Clausewatch describes what changed. It does not tell you what to do about it, and it is not a substitute for professional advice.
  • It is not real time. MAS is checked daily, so a change reaches you within a day of publication — not within minutes.
  • It can miss things. Changes confined to footnotes may not be reported, and changes invisible to text extraction — formatting, or text inside an image — will not be. Never treat silence as proof that nothing changed.
  • MAS is the source of truth. Where anything here differs from the official publication, the official publication is correct.

What it costs

Free through 31 March 2027. From 1 April 2027, SGD 499/month per licensed entity, or SGD 1,200/month for a consultancy licence covering multiple client entities. Cancel anytime, and you will get at least 30 days' notice before any charge.

What we hold about you

Your email address, your organisation name, and the control references you enter. We do not want and do not store your internal policies, your customers' records, or any personal data belonging to your clients — so the honest answer on a vendor security questionnaire is that we hold none of it. Your data is never sent to any AI service; only public MAS text is processed that way.
